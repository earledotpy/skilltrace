§0 The question from #285

"Diagnostic analytics (learner-facing)" — why am I stuck / why recommended primitives — was deferred in G-Beyond (#280) with a friction-shaped trigger: the first real blocker or failed assessment attempt, or learner demand for the primitive. Lived v2.x trips nothing yet: the real store on 2026-09-16 holds 36 events, one active node, and zero blockers, reviews, attempts, and evidence records. Waiting for friction means the first time these questions matter, no answer exists. So this prototype loans the friction: it builds a sandbox fixture day — never the real store, never the real event log — and renders the answers the primitive would give from it, in the engine's existing vocabulary. The fixture is visibly fabricated everywhere it appears (dashed cards); everything the surface reuses is real vocabulary from shipped code.

§1 The fixture day fabricated on purpose

One learner state, assembled from the real graph's own agents slice: the learner has studied on and off for two weeks, is active on agents.concepts.agent_fundamentals_01, has failed its assessment twice, opened a blocker six days ago, and — the part that needs the deepest back-story — passed a consolidation node eleven days ago, which scheduled all three review-cadence recalls at once, two of which are now overdue. That last sentence is the only way a review backlog can exist before mass passing: one real pass schedules the whole backlog (cadence policy, the one sanctioned automation).

§5 Safety posture the boundaries hold by construction

Nothing here touches the hard boundaries. The primitive answers questions; it never flips pass_node/master_node, never deletes a record, never demotes asserted progress, never gates a human action, and AI review plays no part — the answer is a pure derivation rendered in Mentor-voice sections, exactly like every read-only command today. Evidence stays immutable; the simulated fixture edge lives only in the prototype, never in graph/edges.yaml.
  • Read-only like graph impact: no audit event, exit 0 always, advisory findings only.
  • No new record types: blockers, attempts, reviews, sessions are consumed as they are.
  • No new authority: the surface explains; the learner still acts (and only the learner can pass/master).
  • Fixture hygiene: nothing in this prototype writes to graph/, evidence/, execution/, or policy/, and the real event log is untouched.

§6 Fixture ↔ real study days the trip point is friction-shaped

The deferral trigger was written friction-shaped on purpose, and the fixture proves the shape: the parts of this simulation do not age in together. Each element needs a different kind of real event, not a longer calendar — so no day count can predict when the surface becomes answerable.

The live proof: the real store's 36 events span 2026-07-10 → 2026-09-16 — about 68 days — and trip nothing (zero blockers, zero attempts, zero reviews, one active node). 68 calendar days without friction; a different learner could trip the same trigger on day one. That asymmetry is "friction-shaped, not day-count-shaped," and it is why G-Beyond rejected a day-count trigger.

Corollary for the demand branch: the surface is useful the day friction first appears, but the only way to see it before friction is a fixture like this one. The demand question is therefore answerable only from this artifact — or from lived friction later.

§7 What the simulation says input to the demand verdict — not the verdict

Effect on SkillTrace, if built

One read-only explanation surface (an explain-style command plus "why" annotations on existing recommendations), assembled from shipped derivations — the v1.6 themes, v2.1 overlay, v2.2 advisory conventions, remediation/cadence policy values. No new record type, no new store, no new authority, one Mentor-voice section family. On an empty store it honestly declines ("nothing to diagnose yet") in refusal copy — the same posture as today's Mentor facts.

What it buys

On the fixture's bad day, the learner's two real questions get answers that enforce the existing contracts while explaining them: why the wall is a wall (hard prerequisite), why the detour is legal (soft edge), why the review is shouting (cadence + decay), and what passing would actually change (one derived flip). Nothing else in the engine answers "why this, why now" as a direct question today.

What it costs

A new read-only command and its copy to maintain; renderings on every Tier 1 surface that shows recommendations (or a deliberate CLI-only cut at first); and the standing risk of explanation drift from the derivations it quotes — mitigated the same way v2.2 kept graph impact honest: pure core, thin renderer, tables tested against the same fixtures.

The demand question, stated plainly

The fixture shows the primitive is coherent, small, and safe. It cannot show whether it is wanted before real friction exists — that judgment is the learner's, and this artifact is its input. If the rendered bad-day answers make the primitive wanted, the learner-demand branch of the diagnostics trigger (G-Beyond #280) trips: a new verdict on the deferral, candidacy via the roadmap process — never an automatic slot.
